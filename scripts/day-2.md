## 2. Dwindle
**Clock:** 1:06–2:18 · **167 words · 72s**

*[stage] Contrast overlapping windows they grew up with. Per-workspace, sticks across reboot.*

### You say

The default layout is dwindle. Every window on the workspace stays visible. You open a second one, they share. You open a third, they shrink. A fourth, they shrink again. Nothing hides under anything. There is no stack of dirty plates. There is no where-did-Slack-go. You can see the whole table.

That is the entire idea, and it sticks per workspace across reboot. Workspace one can be a browser and a note. Workspace two can be a forest of terminals. Each workspace remembers how it dwindled. You are not fighting a window manager that wants to maximize everything and make you mash Alt plus Tab like it is still 2004.

Hyprland is tiling the windows. Quickshell is drawing the chrome. Dwindle tells the truth: if it is on this workspace, you should be able to see it. If that feels cramped, good. That is last night's acquired taste. Open a couple of windows the way we did yesterday and look. They did not pile. They made room.

## 3. Super+J

*[stage] Demo if you can: two windows, Super+J, again, then Super+Shift+Right to swap.*

### You say

Two windows. Super plus J. Watch the split flip. Side by side, or stacked. Super plus J again. Back the other way. That is the reorient key. You are not dragging a seam. You are telling dwindle which way the cut goes.

When the cut is right but the windows are in the wrong chairs, Super plus Shift plus arrow swaps them. Super plus Shift plus Right. Left. Up. Down. The focused window trades places with the neighbor in that direction.

This is the first navigation drill, and it is almost rude how little there is to it. Super plus J to reorient. Super plus Shift plus arrow to swap. Do it until your hands are bored. Bored hands are fluent hands.

Mac people: this is not Split View. Windows people: this is not Snap. The layout is already tiling. Super plus J just turns the cut.
